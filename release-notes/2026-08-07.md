# Askalot Platform Release — 2026-08-07

## Highlights

- **Sample quality was one number that summed two unrelated failures. It is now four readings that tell you which one happened.** A pool selected off-target and a fielding run that drifted further used to produce the same score, so you could see that something was wrong but not what to fix. The quality view now traces one sample across its whole life: **Strategy → Pool** (did sampling achieve the design?), **Strategy → Actual** (how far off was the realised base before weighting?), and **Strategy → Weighted** (did weighting recover the design?). All three are graded against the same design, which is what puts them on one scale — so the last two differ by *exactly* what weighting recovered, and the step where the distortion entered is the step you act on.

- **A fourth reading isolates fielding itself.** **Pool → Actual** compares who answered against the pool they were drawn from, so a pool that was already off-target is separated from fielding that moved the sample. It is deliberately measured against a different reference than the three above and should be read as a diagnostic, never as a fourth point on the same scale.

- **Your calibration targets are no longer used to grade the weighting they instruct.** The weighted score previously compared the result against the very targets the weighting aimed at — which a successful run hits almost by definition, so it mostly measured whether the algorithm converged rather than whether your sample resembles your research design. It is now graded against the design itself. **Expect scores to move**, and in some cases to move a long way: a weighting that targeted none of your design's factors used to score perfectly and now says so plainly instead. If you have cited a quality score from a previous run, re-open that dataset and read the current figures before relying on it again.

- **A number is never invented where a measurement could not be made.** Where a reading cannot be taken — no design assigned, no pool to compare against, no factor that could be resolved in the data — the view now names the gap and the reason for it. Previously such cases could surface as a score of zero, which reads as "measured, and terrible" rather than "not measured at all". Nothing is averaged over a gap, and the AI Analyst reports the gaps rather than interpolating across them.

- **Partial responses can now count, and you decide where the line falls.** Two different bases are now named wherever a count appears: the **measurement base** is everything fielding returned, and the **analysis base** is the subset that passes a completeness threshold you set per dataset. Completeness is measured as the questions a respondent answered out of the questions they actually reached, so skip logic never reads as someone failing to answer. You can also admit partial and engine-flagged responses into extraction at all, which previously were excluded with no way to opt in.

- **The responses your threshold removes are profiled against the ones it keeps.** Two row counts cannot tell you whether a cut introduced bias, so the excluded group is compared against the retained group on your design's own factors. A completeness cut that removed people who look like the ones it kept is benign; one that did not is a bias you introduced after fielding, and it is now visible rather than silent. An empty excluded set is reported as empty, not as a divergence of zero.

- **The AI Analyst's suggested Brief edits now reach you. Until this release, every one of them was discarded.** When an analysis finished, it would propose specific revisions to your Research Brief — corrections to stated goals, gaps it found in your instrument, claims in the Brief its own findings contradicted. Those proposals were rejected on arrival by a formatting mismatch between what the Analyst produced and what the review queue accepted, so the pending-proposals panel had never shown a single entry for anyone. It now fills as intended, and each proposal arrives with its supporting reasoning for you to accept or reject. **If you have run analyses before and wondered why that panel was always empty, this is why** — re-run an analysis on a Bundle whose Brief you care about to get its proposals.

- **You can see what the analysis is doing while it runs.** A quality analysis can take many minutes, and it previously showed a single unchanging spinner for the entire time — indistinguishable from a run that had silently wedged. It now streams a live activity log: which source it is consulting, which check it is running, and how long each step took. A long run and a stuck one no longer look the same.

- **Reloading the page no longer loses a running analysis.** Navigating away, refreshing, or losing your connection used to leave the analysis running invisibly on the server while the page came back offering to start a new one — and starting it meant two analyses writing to the same dataset, with the last to finish silently overwriting the other. The page now rejoins the run in progress, and a second attempt attaches to the existing analysis instead of duplicating it.

- **Cancel now stops the analysis.** Previously it reported success and the run continued to completion in the background, still consuming your provider quota and still overwriting your existing report at the end. Cancelling now genuinely halts the work, and a cancelled run is reported as cancelled rather than as a failure — and leaves your previous report untouched.

## Fixes & improvements

- **Progress indicators resolve correctly when several steps run at once.** Overlapping steps could close each other's indicators, leaving one spinning permanently after its work had finished.
- **The direct-LLM setting no longer names a model you never chose.** A leftover built-in default meant an unconfigured slot displayed a specific provider and model as though selected, and errors then reported a missing credential for a provider you had never picked. An unconfigured slot now says so plainly and points at the setting to fill in, completing the change described in the previous release.
- **Datasets created before this release need re-extracting to use the completeness features.** They were extracted without the per-response completeness measure, so deriving from one is refused with an explanation rather than quietly applying a threshold to data that cannot support it. Re-extract the raw stage and the rest of the pipeline follows. Existing datasets are otherwise left exactly as they are — nothing was invalidated on your behalf.
- **Each campaign's contribution to the fielded sample is broken out**, including any respondents who took part without belonging to a campaign, so distortion can be attributed to a specific wave rather than to the sample as a whole.
- **The recovery figure is reported in comparable units.** How much distance from your design the weighting closed is now expressed in error terms rather than as a percentage change in the score, so it can be compared across datasets. Where the two sides share no comparable factor it is reported as "not comparable" instead of as zero — which would have read as "weighting achieved nothing".
- **The pipeline board shows at a glance which stages are usable.** A solid outline now means one thing only: a complete, current dataset you can analyse or export. Anything not yet extracted, still running, failed, or gone stale is outlined dashed, and each stage keeps its own colour so a partly-built pipeline still reads at a glance.
