# Askalot Platform Release — 2026-11-22

Rating questions can now declare their scale, so every part of Askalot reads an answer the same way, in every language.

## Highlights

- **Declared rating scales.** A Radio or Dropdown question can say what scale it uses instead of listing its labels: `scale: {family: Agreement, points: 5}`. Six families are available (Agreement, Satisfaction, Likelihood, Frequency, Importance, Quality) at 4, 5 or 7 points. You can show the scale from the top down with `order: descending`, and offer "Don't know", "Does not apply to me" or "Prefer not to say". The labels come in English, Spanish, French, German and Hungarian. You can still write your own wording.
- **One meaning for every code.** A declared scale always stores 1 for its most negative point, whichever way it is shown. Opt-out answers are stored separately (97, 98, 99). They count as answered, but they are never scored as a point of the scale.
- **Analysis reads the scale.** On declared Agreement questions, the acquiescence measure now compares agreeing answers with disagreeing ones. The quality measures leave opt-outs out of every value. SPSS exports mark declared scales as ordinal with opt-outs as missing values, R exports read opt-outs as missing, and the Excel schema sheet names each column's scale.
- **Scale scores in Gold.** The Gold page can combine questions that share a scale into one score (mean or sum), with reverse-worded questions turned around. It reports Cronbach's alpha for the score.

## Fixes & improvements

- The editor suggests a declaration when a question lists the labels of a known scale. It warns when a condition such as `q.outcome >= 4` would also let a "Don't know" answer through.
- The acquiescence measure now counts only labels that actually state agreement.
- An imported questionnaire keeps its printed codes. A scale is declared only when the codes already match it, and the import report lists any it left undeclared.
- The knowledge base explains rating scales in all five languages.
