# Askalot Platform Release — 2026-08-15

## Highlights

- **Your research paper is written by agents, a chapter at a time — and until this release you had no way to notice when one of them changed.** A chapter in the middle could be rewritten between two visits and nothing said so; the paper simply read differently than you remembered, if you happened to re-read that far. There is now a **"Where the research stands"** view beside the paper that answers two questions at a glance: where each chapter stands *for you*, and what has happened to the paper over time.

- **Each chapter is a card, coloured by what it needs from you.** Amber means the words changed since you last read it. Purple means the prose is identical but the data underneath it moved — a distinction worth keeping separate, because one asks you to re-read and the other asks you to re-check your figures. Green means you have read the current version. Grey means nothing has been written there yet, and the card tells you what would fill it and which part of your work produces it. The colour is never the only signal: the two states that ask something of you also say so in words, and a legend names the scheme.

- **A chapter is marked read when you actually read it, not when you open the page.** Opening the paper and closing it immediately clears nothing. The mark moves when a chapter has genuinely been on your screen, so the signal keeps meaning something instead of being wiped by a glance. If your browser cannot support this, nothing is cleared at all — the view is allowed to be over-cautious, never to quietly erase the thing it exists to show you.

- **The first time you open a paper, nothing is flagged.** You are not shown thirteen chapters demanding attention because the paper existed before the feature did. From that first visit onward, anything written into it is tracked.

- **A "What happened" timeline sits underneath, grouped by the run that caused it.** An analysis that wrote five sections appears as one entry naming those five, not as five separate events — so the history reads as a record of work done rather than a log of writes. It keeps the record permanently, including for chapters you have since read and cleared.

- **Every export stage now says what it changed, and shows you the rows.** Each stage of the pipeline previously reported a row count and nothing else, so a stage that dropped rows or added columns was visible only as a number that differed from the one above it. Each stage now states what it introduced compared with the stage it derives from, and opens to a preview of its actual rows in place — the separate dataset page it used to send you to is gone, along with the round trip.

- **You choose which columns stay put while you scroll a wide preview.** A locked column moves to the left of the table rather than merely sticking where it sat, so the columns you are comparing end up beside each other instead of separated by everything you skipped. Preview cells show the label you gave a value, not the code stored underneath it.

## Fixes & improvements

- **A stage refuses to show rows from a derive that has since been superseded**, rather than presenting an older run's data beside a newer run's summary.
- **Row counts state the full stored width beside them**, so a preview that is wider than the table you expected is explained rather than surprising.
- **The completeness cut is recorded on the stage that applied it.** How many responses a threshold admitted and excluded is now kept with the run that made the decision, instead of being computed, reported once in passing, and discarded. A run that excluded nobody records zeros, so "excluded nobody" stays distinguishable from a dataset that predates the record.
- **Chapter links from the status board now land on the chapter**, instead of doing nothing.
- **A chapter whose reference material lives at the organization level is no longer reported as empty** when its sources are shared across projects rather than attached to one.
- **The last moments of a reading session are no longer lost.** Closing the tab immediately after reading a chapter used to discard that final mark, so the chapter stayed flagged as unread.
