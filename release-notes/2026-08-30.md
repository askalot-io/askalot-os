# Askalot Platform Release — 2026-08-30

This release is about the difference between testing an instrument and fielding
one. Until now, trying out your own questionnaire meant fielding it badly: you
became a respondent so the record would resolve, and the result was stored as a
sampled case. Keeping design tests out of your analysis depended on somebody
remembering a checkbox. A test run is now a distinct kind of thing that cannot
become data, and two long-standing gaps around who may take part — reviewers
without accounts, and participants nobody sampled — are closed with doors you
can see the bounds of.

## Highlights

- **Previewing a questionnaire no longer creates research data.** A preview runs the real interview engine, so what it shows you is exactly what a respondent would see — but it belongs to nobody, sits in no campaign, and cannot enter a dataset. That last part is structural rather than a setting: there is no option, recipe key, or toggle anywhere that admits a preview into an analysis. Previews are kept and readable alongside your other runs, clearly marked; they are simply not cases. Launch one from the questionnaire page or straight from the editor.

- **You can share an unpublished draft with a reviewer who has no account.** A preview share link opens the real instrument for someone outside your organisation — a colleague, a client, an ethics reviewer — without giving them a login or letting their walk touch your data. Each person who opens it gets their own run, so two reviewers never overwrite each other's answers. Every such link is bounded by a number of runs you set when you create it and always expires, because it discloses a draft that is still changing.

- **A campaign can publish an open link for participants nobody sampled.** Where a study accepts volunteers, this is the standing public door it has been missing: one link, published by the campaign's owner, bounded by a maximum number of arrivals fixed when you publish it, and stoppable at any moment without pausing the campaign itself. It only lets anyone in while the campaign both accepts volunteers *and* is actively fielding, so a link shared earlier stops admitting people the moment either stops being true. Raising the bound mints a new link and stops the old one in the same action, so there is never a second live door you have forgotten about.

- **Whether a study accepts volunteers is a property of the fielding, not the questionnaire.** The same instrument can now run as a closed panel study in one campaign and a street intercept in another — which the previous, instrument-level setting made impossible. It is stated deliberately when a campaign is created, with no default, because defaulting it would set a study's admission policy while looking exactly like a choice somebody made.

- **Your analysis can tell a volunteer from a sampled participant.** Every response records how its participant arrived, written once when the response is created and never recalculated afterwards — pools change, and a marker computed later would quietly rewrite what a months-old interview was. The quality surfaces now report the composition of the base they graded and how much of it arrived through an open link with identity nobody verified, and a bundle can exclude volunteers from its analysis base without hiding that the split exists.

- **Recording a walk-in collects what the study will actually weight on.** Intake is derived from the campaign's own sampling design rather than a fixed demographic list, so what you are asked at the door is exactly what the analysis grades on later, with an advisory reading of how that participant's group stands against the target. Advisory only: the platform reports where the sample stands, you decide whether to proceed. Giving a name is optional.

## Fixes & improvements

- **Three of the four interviewer-workload tools were failing on every call** and now work: assigning respondents to an interviewer, unassigning them, and listing who is still unassigned.
- **"Unassigned" now means the same thing everywhere.** The count an assistant reports and the list the Manage Workload page shows are derived the same way, so the two can no longer disagree about what work is outstanding.
- **Editing a campaign no longer detaches the exact questionnaire version it is fielding.** A campaign pins the version it was published with so an edit cannot change the instrument mid-study; an unrelated campaign edit used to quietly drop that pin.
- **A campaign-less response is no longer recorded as a sampled case.** Previously the only marker available said the participant had been drawn from the study's sample, which was untrue of a design test and of a walk-in alike.
- **The volunteer arrival page shows the questionnaire's name** rather than an internal identifier.
- **An email typed into an open link is stored as the unverified self-report it is**, never as an identity key — so two strangers claiming the same address are recorded as two people rather than silently merged into one.
- **Stopping a public link touches only that link.** Ending anonymous arrivals no longer requires pausing data collection.
- **The authorisation around the anonymous survey routes has been hardened.** No action is needed on your part, and no participant or reviewer sees any difference.
- **Public survey pages answer with their own message when something goes wrong**, instead of a bare server error that gives a participant no idea whether their link is still good.
- **An unavailable rate-limiter no longer closes a campaign's only public door**, and can no longer refuse a participant indefinitely once it recovers.
- **The preview button works in browsers that block pop-ups**, rather than appearing to do nothing.
